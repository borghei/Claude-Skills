# Pricing Research Methods

Detailed reference material for SaaS pricing research methodologies.

## Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter

Four questions asked to 30+ current customers or qualified prospects:

1. At what price would this be so cheap you would question its quality?
2. At what price would this be a great deal?
3. At what price would this start to feel expensive but still acceptable?
4. At what price would this be too expensive to consider?

**Interpretation:** Plot four curves. The intersection of "too cheap" and "too expensive" gives the acceptable range. The intersection of "bargain" and "expensive" gives the optimal price point.

**Minimum sample:** 30 respondents for statistical validity.

## MaxDiff Analysis

Show respondents sets of features and ask which they value most and least. Reveals relative value of each feature for tier allocation decisions.

**When to use:** Deciding which features go in which tier.

**Process:**
1. List 10-15 features being considered for tier allocation
2. Show respondents sets of 4-5 features at a time
3. Ask: "Which do you value most? Which do you value least?"
4. Repeat across multiple sets to cover all features
5. Calculate relative importance scores

## Competitor Benchmarking

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | List direct competitors and alternatives |
| 2 | Record published pricing (plans, prices, value metrics) |
| 3 | Note what is included at each tier |
| 4 | Identify over-delivery and under-delivery vs each |
| 5 | Position: premium (+20-40%), parity, or value (-10-20%) |

**Rule:** Do not copy competitor prices — their pricing reflects their cost structure and positioning, not yours.

## Customer Willingness-to-Pay Interview

Ask existing customers (especially champions):
- "How would you describe the ROI of [product] to your CFO?"
- "What would you do if the price doubled? Tripled?"
- "What is the most you would pay before you would switch?"
- "If you had to cut 50% of your budget, would this survive?"

## Freemium vs Free Trial Decision

| Factor | Freemium | Free Trial |
|--------|---------|------------|
| Product complexity | Simple, quick time-to-value | Complex, needs exploration |
| Network effects | Strong | Weak |
| Market size | Very large TAM | Focused market |
| Conversion target | 2-5% free-to-paid | 15-30% trial-to-paid |

### Freemium Design Rules
- Free tier must deliver real, ongoing value
- The upgrade trigger should be a natural success indicator
- Free users should preview paid features (soft gates)
- Never remove established free-tier value

## Pricing Page Design Details

### Above the Fold (Required)
- Plan names with clear positioning
- Prices with monthly/annual toggle (default: annual, show "Save 20%")
- 3-5 bullet differentiators per plan
- CTA button per plan
- "Most Popular" badge on middle tier

### Below the Fold
- Full feature comparison table (checkmarks and X marks)
- FAQ section covering 5 objections: cancellation, limits, refunds, security, plan switching
- Social proof: logos, testimonials, case studies
- Security badges: SOC2, ISO 27001, GDPR

### Enterprise Tier
| Approach | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|
| Published price | Standardized enterprise pricing |
| "Contact Sales" | Custom scoping required |
| "Starting at $X" | Balance transparency with flexibility |

## Price Increase Communication Template

**Subject:** "Changes to your [Product] plan"

- **Paragraph 1:** What is changing and when
- **Paragraph 2:** Why (new features, investment, market alignment)
- **Paragraph 3:** What this means specifically (old price → new price)
- **Paragraph 4:** Options (lock in current price with annual, downgrade, contact support)
- **CTA:** "View your options" or "Talk to your account manager"
