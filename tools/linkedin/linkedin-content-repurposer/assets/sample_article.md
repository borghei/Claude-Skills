# What four years of customer support rotas taught me

In this article I want to walk through the changes we made to the support rota at Brackenhall, a 40-person company that makes booking software for dental practices. Dear reader, it is longer than I intended.

## The weekend problem

When I joined in 2022 the weekend rota was filled by whoever replied last in the group chat. Over the first year we logged 212 weekend tickets and 58 of them waited more than twelve hours for a first reply. Nobody had decided that was acceptable. It had just become normal.

I assumed the fix was headcount. We hired a fifth support person in early 2023 and the twelve-hour figure barely moved, because the new hire ended up in the same last-to-reply lottery as everyone else. The problem was not the number of people. It was that nobody owned Saturday morning.

So we stopped asking for volunteers. Each person now owns one weekend in five, published a quarter ahead. In the following year we logged 240 weekend tickets and 9 waited more than twelve hours.

## The handover note

As mentioned above, ownership fixed the weekend. It did not fix Monday. Tickets opened on Sunday night were being picked up on Monday by someone with no context, and customers were asked the same questions twice.[1]

We introduced a handover note with exactly three lines: what is still open, what I promised and to whom, and what I would do next. I wanted a longer template. The team argued me down, and they were right: a three-line note gets written, and a twelve-field form does not. Within two months repeat-question complaints dropped from 17 a month to 4.

## The on-call swap market

In the next section of our internal write-up we describe the swap board in detail; here is the short version. People were swapping weekends privately and the rota on the wall was wrong about a third of the time. Instead of banning swaps I made them public: a single pinned message where any swap has to be posted before it counts.

I used to think flexibility and predictability pulled against each other. What the swap board showed is that people will accept a fixed rota if they can see a legitimate way out of it. In the six months after the board went up there were 31 swaps and no missed weekend.

## What I would do differently

I would have fixed ownership before hiring. The fifth hire was the right decision eventually, but in 2023 it cost us a salary to learn that our problem was a calendar. I would also have asked the team to design the handover note from the start, since my version was three times too long.
